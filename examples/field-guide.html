Yosemite National Park

Trailhead: Happy Isles

Shuttle stop #16

Vernal Fall and Nevada Fall, by the Mist Trail

A granite stairway of over 600 steps, two waterfalls and two ways back down. You can turn around at the footbridge, at the top of Vernal Fall or at the top of Nevada Fall.

Chapter 2

Before mile 0

Getting to Happy Isles

There is no parking at the trailhead itself. Park at Curry Village, less than 1 mile (1.6 km) away, or ride the Valleywide or East Valley Shuttle to shuttle stop #16. From the stop, follow the signs across the bridge to the trail.

No trailhead parkingCurry Village, under 1 mi

Pets, bicycles and strollers are not allowed on this trail. Fill your bottles at the stop: the trailhead and the footbridge are the only drinking water along the way.

WaterStop #16 and the footbridge

Chapter 3

Mile 0 to 0.8

To the Vernal Fall Footbridge

The footbridge is 0.8 mile (1.3 km) from the trailhead and 400 feet higher. It has an excellent view of Vernal Fall. Turn around here and the round trip is 1.6 miles, rated moderate, one to one and a half hours.

0.8 mi · 1.3 kmWater and restrooms, May to October

From May to October there is water and a restroom at the bridge. It is the last tap on the trail. Past here, you carry what you drink.

Chapter 4

The stairway

Up the Mist Trail to Vernal Fall

About 0.2 mile (0.3 km) beyond the bridge, the Mist Trail and the John Muir Trail part. Take the Mist Trail 0.3 mile (0.5 km) up a steep granite stairway of over 600 steps.

Over 600 steps0.3 mi · 0.5 km

In spring and early summer the fall throws a tremendous amount of spray across the steps, and the footing is slippery. At the top you look straight down the length of the 317-foot waterfall.

Closed in winterLower Mist Trail, for falling ice and rock

The steps can be slick and crowded. To come down, take the cutoff to Clark Point instead.

Chapter 5

Vernal to Nevada

Past Emerald Pool to Nevada Fall

Shortly beyond the brink of Vernal Fall you pass Emerald Pool and Silver Apron. Swimming here is illegal and dangerous because of the extremely hazardous current.

No swimmingEmerald Pool, Silver Apron

The current is the danger. Stay out of the river and off its edge.

From the top of Vernal Fall it is another 1.5 miles (2.4 km) of steep, rocky switchbacks to Nevada Fall, which crashes down 594 feet. The round trip from Happy Isles is 5.4 miles with 2,000 feet of climbing, five to six hours.

1.5 mi · 2.4 kmSwitchbacks to Nevada Fall

There are vault toilets along the Mist Trail beyond the top of Vernal Fall, and at the top of Nevada Fall. Some hikers go on from here to Half Dome or into the backcountry.

Vault toiletsAbove Vernal Fall, top of Nevada Fall

Chapter 6

The way back

Two ways down

The Mist Trail is the short way: 2.5 miles (4 km). The John Muir Trail is about 4 miles one way, and longer. From the top of Nevada Fall, continue over the bridge to the John Muir Trail for the alternate route back to Yosemite Valley.

Mist 2.5 miJohn Muir about 4 mi

If you only went as far as Vernal Fall, make a loop: carry on past the brink and Emerald Pool to the next junction, about 0.2 mile (0.3 km), take the cutoff to Clark Point and come down the John Muir Trail.

Whichever way you take, stay on the switchbacks. Cutting them causes erosion and injuries.

Chapter 7

Winter

When the trail closes

Both falls can be reached all year, but two sections close in winter, marked in rust on Fig. 2. The lower Mist Trail along Vernal Fall closes because of falling ice and rock. The upper John Muir Trail between Clark Point and the top of Nevada Fall closes because of treacherously icy conditions.

Two closuresLower Mist Trail; John Muir Trail above Clark Point

Open sections can still be very icy or snowy. Check current conditions on the park's trail page before you set out.

Chapter 8

Stay safe

What hurts people here

Sprained ankles and knee injuries are common on this trail. The causes are loose rock, decomposed granite, steep steps and wet, slippery footing.

Common injuriesAnkles and knees
  • Stay on the established trail. Do not swim, wade or walk along the river's edge.
  • Observe warning signs and railings. They are there for your safety.
  • Carry a headlamp, with fresh or extra batteries.
  • Drink plenty and drink often, pace yourself, rest in the shade and eat salty snacks.
  • Keep food within arm's reach, never feed wildlife, and pack out all trash and food waste.
